## SHEET 2 — PERSIAN POETRY FORMS & METERS CHEAT SHEET

**Forms:** ghazal = 5–15 couplets (7–12 typical), rhyme aa·ba·ca·da… + optional radif refrain, lyric
tetrameter 14–16 syllables, matla sets rhyme, maqta may carry takhallos; masnavi = long couplets
aa·bb·cc, ONE 11-syllable (or 10) meter throughout, narrative; rubai = 4 hemistichs, AA·BA, two
dedicated 13-syllable patterns (Elwell-Sutton 3.3.13 / 5.1.13) used ONLY for rubai; qet'a = one theme,
no rhyming-matla requirement; qasida = long formal monorhyme; nimayi = free line lengths, rhythmic not
metered; sepid = free, speech rhythm; tarane = V–C pop schemes, syllable-counted.

**Syllable law (verified):** short = ends in short vowel; long = long vowel or consonant ending;
overlong = long+short (e.g. دُوست، رُوز، پَنج); line-final syllable ALWAYS long; never >2 shorts in a row.

**Scan-and-fix loop:** pick meter → draft → COUNT every mesra' → mark u/– on failures → rewrite →

**Qafiye/radif:** qafiye = same final letters + same haraka; radif = identical repeated word(s) AFTER
qafiye; same-word repetition ≠ rhyme; ghazal: matla sets it, later couplets' 2nd lines follow.

**Dastgah (names verified; moods conventional ~):** شور longing/introspective (+avaz: ابوعطا، دشتی،
افشاری، بیات‌ترک) · سه‌گاه mystical · نوا calm · همایون majestic (+آواز اصفهان romantic) · چهارگاه
epic · ماهور bright/major-like · راست‌پنجگاه noble. Radif codified by Mirza Abdollah (1843–1918);
UNESCO Intangible Heritage 2009.

## SHEET 3 — RHYME & FLOW TECHNIQUES CHEAT SHEET

**End-rhyme schemes:** AABB couplets (self-contained punches) · ABAB (flow) · ABCB (ballad) · AAAA
(drive — unfolds fast) · ABBA (enclosed) · XAXA (sparse) · chain rhyme (carry rhyme across stanzas) ·
monorhyme (AAAA… whole section — use briefly). Slant/near rhyme OK in verses; TRUE rhyme in chorus +
final line. Persian classical: ghazal aa·ba·ca (+radif) · masnavi aa·bb·cc · rubai AA·BA.

**Rhyme density tools:** internal rhyme (mid-line pairs) · multisyllabic rhyme (FA: ـارم/ـارت/ـاره
chains; EN: "prayer/fare" → "player/mayor") · assonance chains (vowel echo) · consonance (consonant
echo) · alliteration bursts (≤1 line each) · refrain word (unrhymed anchor repeated for rhythm).

**Flow (rap & sung rhythm):** bar = 1 line; keep bar syllables within ±2 per verse; switch cadence
(syllables-per-beat) every 4 bars; enjambment = speed, end-stopped = weight; punchline = 3 setup bars +
payoff (last word hits hardest); wordplay ≤1 per 4 bars; breath rule (one breath per bar); rests are
flow too (half-bar + pause). Persian: stress colloquial naturally (نِمیشه، می‌خوام) — never force formal
stress into a rap bar.

**Meter matching (sung):** verses ±1 syllable; chorus EXACT; downbeats carry nouns/verbs, never
را/و/که/unstressed particles; open vowels (a/â/o) on held notes; short vowels on fast runs; test every
line against a metronome mentally.

**Hook engineering:** ≤8 words; singable by a crowd; repeat 2–3× with a lift (melody/energy, not just
volume); title inside or adjacent; post-chorus chant = 1–2 words ×4.

## SHEET 4 — BANNED-CLICHÉ LIST (use only if reinvented beyond recognition)

**EN — romance:** fire/desire · dance in the moonlight · broken wings · drowning in your love ·
cold as ice / hot as fire · reach for the stars · written in the stars · against all odds · at the end
of the day · my heart skips a beat · tears in rain (film-bound) · like a bird set free.
**EN — genre traps:** neon lights + midnight drive (synthwave) · whiskey + pain + dirt road (country) ·
put your hands up (EDM) · grind/hustle flex with no detail (trap) · "yeah yeah" ×8 as a whole chorus.
**FA — romance:** آتیش عشق · مثل پروانه (در پاپ) · دریای چشمات · ستاره‌ی بخت · گل و بلبل (در ترانه‌ی
مدرن) · «قلبم وایساد» · «تو دنیای منی» (بی‌تصویر) · «نفسم بند اومد» (تکرار بی‌تصویر) · مهتاب/مهتاب‌شب
(در پاپ، مگر بازآفرینی) · «قول میدم تا ابد».
**FA — craft traps:** قافیه‌ی هم‌واژه (بیدارم/بیدارم) · ردیفِ سوارشده بر قافیه‌ی سست · قاطی رسمی/محاوره‌ای
در یک آهنگ · برساخت انگلیسی («تو را حس می‌کنم»، «می‌خواهم تو بدانی که…») · اضافه‌ی جاافتاده در هوک.
**Rescue rule:** a cliché survives ONLY with a concrete twist that changes its meaning
("cold as ice" → "cold as the hospital chair" — specific, earned).
